The market for AI training work has grown fast, and so has the number of platforms offering it. If you're trying to figure out where to start — or which platforms are worth your time — this breakdown will help.

Here are 10 of the best platforms to get paid for AI work right now, based on task variety, pay, and ease of getting started.

## 1. Mercor

**Best for:** Beginners and people who want flexible, varied AI tasks

Mercor has become one of the more popular entry points for AI work. The platform offers a range of task types, and the onboarding process is relatively straightforward. Pay is competitive for the task difficulty level.

## 2. Outlier

**Best for:** Writers, researchers, and people with subject-matter expertise

Outlier tends to pay more than many other platforms, especially for tasks that require domain knowledge. If you have a background in a specific field — science, law, history, coding — you may qualify for higher-paying task categories.

## 3. Data Annotation Tech

**Best for:** People who want consistent, predictable work

Data Annotation Tech is known for offering more consistent work availability than some other platforms. The tasks are generally straightforward, and the onboarding process is clear. A good choice if you want something reliable.

## 4. Scale AI

**Best for:** People who want variety

Scale AI offers a wide range of annotation and evaluation projects. Task availability can vary, but the platform is well-established and has been around longer than most. Worth having an account even if you don't use it as your primary platform.

## 5. Telus

**Best for:** People who want a large, established platform

Telus (formerly Lionbridge AI) is one of the biggest names in the space. The platform has been operating for years and offers a wide variety of tasks. Onboarding can take longer than newer platforms, but the stability is worth it.

## 6. Mindrift

**Best for:** Creative writers and people who enjoy open-ended tasks

Mindrift focuses on creative AI training tasks — writing stories, generating dialogue, crafting prompts. If you enjoy writing and want tasks that feel more engaging than standard annotation, this is a strong option.

## 7. Toloka

**Best for:** International applicants and people who want fast onboarding

Toloka is available in many countries and has a relatively fast onboarding process. Task variety is good, and the platform is straightforward to navigate. A solid choice if you're outside the US and finding other platforms harder to access.

## 8. Neevo AI

**Best for:** People interested in voice and language tasks

Neevo specializes in data collection tasks including voice recordings, translations, and language-specific annotation. If you speak multiple languages or are interested in linguistics-related work, Neevo is worth checking out.

## 9. Alignerr

**Best for:** People with technical or academic backgrounds

Alignerr focuses on AI alignment work — evaluating whether AI responses are accurate, helpful, and safe. Tasks tend to require more careful thinking, and pay reflects that. A good fit if you have a strong analytical background.

## 10. Surge

**Best for:** People who want clean, well-organized task interfaces

Surge is known for having a clean platform and well-structured tasks. It's a smaller platform than some others on this list, but the quality of the task design is consistently good.

## Tips for getting the most out of these platforms

**Apply to several at once.** Work availability fluctuates on every platform. Having accounts on three to five platforms gives you more consistent income and more options when one platform is slow.

**Take the qualification tests seriously.** Most platforms require you to pass a test before accessing tasks. These tests are often the hardest part of the process — read the instructions carefully and don't rush.

**Track your accuracy.** On most platforms, your accuracy rate affects what tasks you're offered and how much you earn. Prioritize quality over speed, especially when you're starting out.

August marked my first serious push into the AI training and data-annotation space. It quickly became clear that this industry rewards persistence, patience, and a willingness to take small steps before larger opportunities appear.

## CrowdGen
I completed one generalist audio task. The pay was minimal, but the value was in establishing activity on the platform. Early completions help unlock better projects later.

## DataAnnotation
I skipped the Generalist test and jumped straight into the Developer test. This permanently locked me out of the Generalist track. Lesson learned: always start with the broad qualification first.

## Outlier
I passed the Generalist test but failed onboarding for two projects. Outlier's tests are demanding, and gaining more hands-on experience before trying again is the better strategy.

## Mercor
Mercor sent an update: 4 out of my 11 applications were marked "not hired." These were project-specific rejections, not skill-based, so I still have access to future opportunities. I plan to take Mercor's assessments to improve my placement chances.

## Mindrift
I applied to Mindrift and submitted my CV. Mindrift has merged with Toloka, and the unified platform will host their annotation and training projects. Mindrift emailed me with a project offer, but the hourly wage was extremely low. I am taking the project anyway to build a record of completed tasks and unlock better opportunities later.

## Closing Thoughts
August was about positioning myself. September will be about qualifying, completing, and building momentum.

You've probably heard that AI is changing the job market. What you might not know is that AI companies are actively paying everyday people to help build and improve their models — and most of them don't require any technical experience to get started.

This guide covers exactly what AI training work involves, who's hiring, and how to land your first task.

## What does "training AI" actually mean?

When AI companies say they need people to "train" their models, they usually mean one of a few things:

- **Rating AI responses** — You're shown a prompt and two AI-generated answers, and you pick which one is better (or explain why neither is good).
- **Writing training prompts** — You write questions, instructions, or scenarios that the AI will learn from.
- **Data annotation** — You label images, transcribe audio, or tag text so the AI can understand patterns.
- **Reviewing AI outputs** — You check whether an AI's answer is accurate, helpful, and safe.

None of these tasks require you to know how to code. They require you to read carefully, think critically, and communicate clearly.

## Who is hiring right now?

There are more than 17 platforms actively recruiting for this kind of work. Some of the most well-known include:

- **Mercor** — flexible AI task work, good for beginners
- **Outlier** — writing and reasoning tasks, often pays more for subject-matter expertise
- **Data Annotation Tech** — consistent weekly work, straightforward onboarding
- **Scale AI** — variety of annotation and evaluation projects
- **Telus** — one of the largest and most established platforms in the space
- **Mindrift** — creative AI training tasks, good for writers

Most of these platforms are remote, available worldwide (or in many countries), and let you set your own hours.

## How to get started

**Step 1: Pick two or three platforms to apply to.**
Don't apply to all 17 at once. Start with a few that match your background. If you're a strong writer, Outlier and Mindrift are good starting points. If you want something more structured, Data Annotation Tech or Telus are worth trying.

**Step 2: Complete the onboarding test.**
Most platforms require a short qualification test before you can access tasks. These tests check whether you can follow instructions and evaluate quality. Take your time — the test is often the hardest part.

**Step 3: Start with smaller tasks.**
Once you're in, start with lower-complexity tasks to get a feel for the platform's expectations. Your accuracy rate matters on most platforms, so it's better to go slow and get it right.

**Step 4: Apply to more platforms over time.**
Work availability varies by platform and fluctuates week to week. Having accounts on multiple platforms gives you more consistent income.

## What to expect in terms of pay

Pay varies significantly depending on the platform and the type of task. Simple annotation tasks might pay a few dollars per hour. More specialized work — like evaluating AI responses in a specific domain (law, medicine, coding) — can pay considerably more.

Most people treat this as a side income stream rather than a primary salary. That said, some people do earn meaningful income from it, especially once they've qualified for higher-paying task types.

## Is this legitimate?

Yes. These are real companies with real products. Mercor, Outlier, Scale AI, and Telus are well-funded, established businesses. Data annotation and AI instruction work is a genuine industry that's growing as AI development accelerates.

That said, as with any gig work, there's no guarantee of consistent hours or income. Treat it as a flexible side income, not a stable salary.
